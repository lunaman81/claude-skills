# PR-FAQ (Press Release + FAQ)

The PR-FAQ is Amazon's format for new products and features. You write the press release *before* building, plus the FAQs that explain the tradeoffs. If you can't write a compelling press release, the product probably isn't worth building. The form forces you to start from the customer outcome and work backwards.

## When to use

- Proposing a new product or feature
- Killing or deprecating a feature (yes — write the obit)
- Major pivots or repositioning

## Structure

### Part 1: The Press Release (1 page max)

Pretend the product launched today. Write the press release announcing it.

1. **Headline.** Specific and customer-benefit framed. Not "Openly Launches AI Tool." Instead: "Openly Now Shows Real Roof Replacement Quotes from Local Installers in 30 Cities."

2. **Subheadline.** One sentence. Who it's for and why they care.

3. **Location and date.** Treat it as real — "Austin, TX — June 1, 2026."

4. **Summary paragraph.** What the product is and the problem it solves. 3-4 sentences.

5. **Problem paragraph.** Describe the pain in concrete terms, with a specific customer or scenario. Not "homeowners struggle with pricing" — instead "A Dallas homeowner replacing a 2,400 sq ft roof today sees national-average estimates ranging from $8,000 to $40,000 with no way to know which applies to her house."

6. **Solution paragraph.** How the product solves it. Specific, not feature-listy.

7. **Customer quote.** A made-up but plausible quote from a real type of customer, with a name and city. If you can't write a believable one, the value isn't clear.

8. **How to get started.** What does the customer do, today, to use it? Be specific — URL, action, time.

9. **Leader quote.** The CEO or product lead, articulating the thesis and why it matters strategically.

### Part 2: External FAQs (5-10)

What customers will ask. Pricing, availability, comparison to alternatives, what it doesn't do, how it works, privacy, support.

Each FAQ is one question and one paragraph. Don't dodge — if a question has an awkward answer, give the awkward answer.

### Part 3: Internal FAQs (5-15)

What the team will ask. The internal FAQs are where the real argument happens — most teams underweight them.

Cover at minimum:
- What's the cost to build and operate?
- What are the technical risks?
- What dependencies do we have?
- What alternatives did we consider, and why did we reject them?
- What could kill this — what would have to be true for this to fail?
- How will we know it's working in 30/60/90 days?
- What's the long-term maintenance burden?

## Length

- **PR:** ~1 page, no more
- **External FAQs:** 1-2 pages
- **Internal FAQs:** 2-5 pages — the hard thinking lives here

## Failure modes specific to PR-FAQs

- **Vague PR.** "Openly is excited to announce a powerful new platform that helps homeowners save time and money." Replace every clause with specifics or it isn't useful.
- **Soft internal FAQs.** "Q: Is there any risk? A: We've thought about it." If the FAQs don't surface real fear, you haven't done the work. The reader should be able to find the strongest argument *against* in the internal FAQs.
- **Skipped customer quote.** It feels silly to write fake quotes. Write it anyway. If you can't write a believable one, that's the signal — the customer's gain isn't clear yet.
- **Feature list disguised as solution.** The solution paragraph turns into a bulleted feature list. It's a press release, not a spec sheet. Describe what the customer experiences, not what the system does.
